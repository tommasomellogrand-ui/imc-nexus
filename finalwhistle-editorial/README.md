# Final Whistle editorial line — approved by Tom

Every completed match follows the same editorial sequence:

1. Masolin introduces the result and a decisive theme, recalling Coffee Time.
2. The home correspondent analyzes the actual performance and interviews the
   home manager about episodes, tactics, players and the next relevant objective.
3. The away correspondent provides the opposing perspective and interviews
   the away manager. Neither side is omitted, including eliminated teams.
4. Condò explains context; Costacurta analyzes defending; Del Piero analyzes
   attacking play and protagonists; Masolin closes the segment.

Use the exact correspondents and managers assigned in the Coffee edition.
Avoid repeated score summaries, generic questions disconnected from the game,
invented tactical changes and unsupported qualification claims. Dialogue is
fictional reconstruction under the programme's existing visible disclosure.

For 27 October, the Hertha–Parma script preserves the approved full example;
all three other quarter-finals have equally structured original interviews.
There are four questions per manager, eight interviews and 32 answers.
Kaiserslautern–Roma ended 1–1 and 5–4 on penalties. The penalty fields are null
in the source, but the full shootout is explicitly described in events_json.
The narrative and penalty label are tied to the SHA-256 of the report evidence.

The automatic Coffee/Final Whistle linkage remains active. Before a report
exists, interview blocks remain absent. New or corrected reports use the
editorial structure automatically. The approved current scripts display only
while their report fingerprints match, preventing stale narratives after
corrections. Raw event_type=goal is unreliable: goals come from normalized
scorers, not from that label (which also occurs on saves/offside/shootouts).

Publication changes only Final Whistle JS and the landing HTML. Coffee Time,
Results, Schedule, Match Reports and other programmes are read-only.
